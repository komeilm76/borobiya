# User manual

The user application is a right-to-left mobile experience served from the same origin as the API. It works in a browser and can be installed through the web app manifest. Offline static assets and map tiles remain available through the service worker; private account data requires a signed-in connection.

## Account access

Start at `/_auth/login`. Enter your username and password; the primary button becomes active when the form is valid. Use the password visibility control if needed. New accounts are created through registration with five distinct security questions and answers. Recovery begins with a username lookup, continues through three selected questions and ends with a new password. A successful recovery signs out all existing sessions. Administrator-created accounts complete the same five security questions during onboarding before normal use.

Theme follows the system by default. You can explicitly choose light or dark. The choice is applied before the first paint so the screen does not flash. Active devices can be reviewed and revoked from the profile section; login history is available for auditing your own account.

## Loading, offline and keyboard access

The shell shows a loading skeleton while a view is fetched, an offline panel when navigation requires a route that has not been cached, and a retryable error panel if a view cannot be loaded. The service worker caches application routes and static assets; map tiles use a bounded offline cache. On reconnect, the realtime client resumes durable event synchronization instead of asking you to reload. Use the skip link at the start of the page to jump directly to the main content, and press `Ctrl` or `Command` plus `K` to open the command palette.

## Map and places

The map is the home surface. Use the five-part top selector to move between history, map, trips, sharing and mission. The search field finds destinations, and quick chips jump to saved places. The layers and compass controls adjust the view; recenter returns to your location. An accuracy circle shows how precise the current fix is. Live positions you are allowed to see appear as ringed markers with freshness indicators.

A destination is selected by tapping the map: the sheet shows its coordinates and, with a fix available, the distance, and can save it as a place (name, label, address and icon). Saved places are listed, edited inline and deleted from the profile section. Every place has a name, label, coordinates, address, icon and rank; reordering exists as an API but not as UI yet. Live location sharing can be granted to another account for a bounded duration, without any expiry until you revoke it, or for a historical range with an explicit detail scope, and can be revoked at any time. The history screen groups past records by day in three tabs. Trips lists your finished trips with their ranked stop list, and offers repeat-as-new-trip and save; Missions splits between your requests (with the server timeline of each request) and the missions you took (with the rating and note the requester gave); Shares audits every location share you were part of — active, expired or revoked. Any record expands inline and can spawn a history-window share with a detail scope; the default range is the record's own lifetime. There is no replay surface: location history is only ever readable through an active, scoped share.

## Teams and support requests

Missions that are dispatched as a team carry a staffing block on the mission screen. It shows the leader (highlighted), every active and removed member, the required composition with a filled-versus-requested counter per job type and the pending support-request count. If you are not a member of the mission team the block explains that instead of rendering the roster.

The team leader additionally sees a compose-support button. It opens a form listing the catalog of active job types; choose the missing types and quantities, optionally add a note, and send a support request to the administrators — it is created in pending state. While it waits you can withdraw it; withdrawal is a two-step confirmation, exactly like other destructive actions. Administrators review the request, pick the workers (they see who is eligible and who is not, with the reason), and approve it fully, approve it partially or reject it. If you are not the leader or the requester you never see the internal review note; the leader and the requester do.

## Trips and group rides

Trips are ordered routes you can save and share. Open the trips screen from the top selector or the sheet-footer trip button on the map. It starts with the invitations inbox: when someone you know invites you to a trip, accept or decline from here — accepting joins you to the trip, declining leaves it out. The list below shows your trips with their status (planned, active, completed or cancelled), your role (leader or member), and the stop and member counts; filter to saved trips only if you want. Tick the star on a row to save or unsave a trip.

Create a trip from the new-trip form — name it, then add at least one stop. Each stop has a label and coordinates; you can type them, drop a stop on the current map centre, or insert a saved place from the place picker. Stops are ranked in the order you add them, and the creation flow posts them as an ordered list. The trip row opens the detail view: the ranked stop list, the member roster with roles, and the leader's and your own actions. A trip's leader manages it: rename, wholesale stop replacement, start, complete, cancel, and invitations. Starting moves a planned trip to active; completing or cancelling closes it. An invited account must explicitly accept before it appears in the roster — the leader cannot add anyone unilaterally. Members can leave a trip at any time; the leader cannot leave one — they complete or cancel it. Non-members are answered with a not-found response rather than a forbidden one, so the existence of someone else's trip is never confirmed. A finished trip remains readable but rejects every mutation. Saved and unsaved state is per account.

The trips screen does not compute routes: there is no turn-by-turn guidance, no ETA and no traffic, by design. Navigation runs on the configured offline map; the trip is the shared plan, not a GPS engine.

### Recorded tracks and replay

Recording a track is always opt-in, twice: first on the profile screen (the "Record track" switch, off by default, account-wide), then per context — when you start (or join) a recorded trip or mission, the app asks once whether to record that context; declining is remembered and never asked again, and you can switch the account toggle off at any time (a recording indicator above the map shows "Recording trip/mission …" with a stop button while it is on). Only the trips you join and the missions you take part in can be recorded; nothing else is ever written. Points worse than 100 m accuracy are dropped.

Finished trips and missions expose replay on their history detail: the recorded distance and time (from the server's summary, never estimated), a per-member breakdown, and a schematic path view. Availability is stated explicitly — "recorded" (available), "expired" (raw points were purged after the retention window; the summary survives) or "none" (recording never ran). Trip and mission views show the same availability chip. Shares with a window covering the context let a history-share recipient replay only the sharer's points, under the granted scope (route always; heading/speed and accuracy only if granted), until the grant expires or is revoked; no one else sees your track, and a foreign or unknown id answers as not-found. Administrators never read tracks silently: the administrator read exists for safeguarding and is always audit-logged.

## SOS and missions

The red SOS dock on the left edge requires a deliberate 0.8-second press-and-hold. The four-step form collects description, location confirmation, an optional photo and a final review; on the location step you can either adopt your current fix or arm tap-to-place and drop the incident pin anywhere on the map (a dashed marker marks a manually pinned location, and the review repeats that the position is manual) — the photo is resized and re-compressed in the browser before it is sent, so a camera frame does not blow past the server's size limit. Severity and incident types come from the server catalog. After submission, a tracking code and progress rail show creation, dispatch, acceptance, response and completion. You can cancel while the state machine permits it using a catalog reason — some reasons ask for a short written explanation (at least ten characters). Dispatchers see the nearest responders with distance, rating and readiness and pick the group and the people explicitly before anything is dispatched. Responders see dispatched requests as an offer card on the mission screen — severity, incident type, accuracy, whether the location was set manually, distance and the requester — and can accept the offer or decline it. A dispatched request never appears on a responder's mission list unless an offer was addressed to that responder (or, for legacy requests created before offers existed, the responder belonged to the target group); accepting an offer of a type with the every-offer policy also marks the responder ready for the next mission. Some operational job types only offer work while you are on standby — if such an offer is missing, check your readiness on the profile screen. Once the mission is complete, the requester can rate it from one to five stars and attach an optional short note for the responders; the note is stored with the rating and shown on the mission card afterwards.

A responder follows the mission rail through travel, arrival, response and completion. A requester can rate a completed mission once. The same durable events update the browser, WebSocket subscribers and administrator dispatch board.

## Sharing and talk

The Sharing Center lists everyone sharing a position with you and everyone you share with. Rows show freshness, presence and sharing state, and each share lists the counterpart and the direction of the share; a direction filter (all, outgoing, incoming) narrows the list. Outgoing shares can be revoked from the same list at any time. Use the new-share form to search an account by username, pick the recipient, then grant either a live share (15 minutes, 1, 4 or 24 hours, or no expiry until you revoke it) or a history window (from/to, at most 30 days back). A history share carries an explicit detail scope: the route points are always included, and you choose whether movement details (heading and speed) and position accuracy are shared too; the recipient never receives more than the granted scope, and both sides can see what the scope is. The created share appears in the active-share list and refreshes automatically. Sharing and revocation are recorded in the audit log. One-to-one talk is press-and-hold: hold the amber microphone while speaking and release to stop. Only the current speaker sends audio, and the panel explicitly states that no audio is recorded. If the connection drops, the app reconnects with a fresh ticket and catches up through the durable event cursor.

## Profile

The fifth section contains account details, username changes, responder mission readiness, security readiness, devices, login history, saved places and sign-out. Responders get a readiness switch on this screen: dispatchers see it as ready/busy on the dispatch board. Recent logins (device, IP, success or failure) are listed at the bottom. Sign-out revokes the current session and closes the realtime connection. If an administrator deactivates the account, all sessions are revoked and the browser returns to login.
